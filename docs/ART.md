# Art - The Herbal Witch

Palette, material and tint rules, pixel density, model budgets, provenance, and the art-spike record.
Reference: the owner's concept sheet (warm dark wood, cream, brass, deep green / plum / rose
upholstery, small floral motifs, restrained greenery). It is a palette and silhouette guide, not a
mesh specification.

## Rules

- **Materials** (`textures/block/material/`, 32 x 32, generated by `tools/generate-textures.mjs`):
  tinted neutrals `wood_grain`, `painted_wood`, `cut_stone`, `fabric_floral`, `rug`, `lamp_shade`;
  untinted `brass`, `glass` (cutout, alpha 0/255), `tincture` (three colour bands picked by UV).
- **Tints:** index 0 = frame colour, 1 = fabric colour, from the finish. Brass, glass and tinctures
  carry no tint index. Block and item colour handlers are registered together.
- **Density:** 32 px material tiles; one UV unit per model pixel (two texels per pixel) on every face
  except glass, which stretches its rim over each face. Herb and item sprites (later) are 16 px.
- **Models:** Blockbench-style cuboid JSON templates in `models/block/decor/templates/`, explicit UVs,
  within one block, axis-aligned. Finish wrappers bind only `frame`. Budget for the spike: 6-16
  elements per piece; keep ordinary furniture under ~30 elements.
- **Render types:** solid by default; `minecraft:cutout` only where a template has glass. No
  translucency yet.
- **Provenance:** all textures are generated from seeded code in `tools/art/`; all models are
  authored for this mod. Nothing is copied from vanilla or other mods. MIT.

## Art spike (Milestone 1) - owner review checklist

Status: **awaiting owner review.** Nothing in Milestone 5 (mass modelling) starts until the findings
below are recorded.

1. In the trial instance (creative), open the **The Herbal Witch: Cottage Decor** tab: 90 items,
   piece by piece, finish by finish. Check every icon reads as its piece and finish.
2. Build a small room: per piece, place at least Dark Oak, Pale Oak, Black, Stone, Plum and Cream
   (the extremes), then sample the rest. Turn armchairs and counters in all four directions.
3. Hang shelves on a wall; stand lamps on the floor and on a counter; switch lamps on at night.
4. Look at: wood and paint colours against the concept swatches; whether tone-on-tone florals read
   as fabric; UV density (do 32 px materials look too fine or too busy next to vanilla blocks?);
   silhouettes (arm height, counter overhang, bottle size); brass amount; lit lamp glow; items in
   hand, on the ground and in item frames.
5. In survival: craft each dark oak base, restyle in a stonecutter, break pieces and check the drop
   keeps its finish.
6. Optional: shaders on/off, a resource reload (F3+T), and a second player watching a lamp switch.

Feedback wanted: per finish "keep / adjust colour (to what)"; tone-on-tone vs multi-colour florals;
32 px vs 16 px materials; per piece "silhouette OK / change (what)"; anything missing from the look.
